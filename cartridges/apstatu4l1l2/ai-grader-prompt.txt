## Key Vocabulary for This Topic
- Random process: A situation where all possible outcomes are known, but individual outcomes are unpredictable
- Outcome: The result of a single trial of a random process
- Event: A collection of outcomes from a random process
- Simulation: A way to model random events such that simulated outcomes closely match real-world outcomes
- Empirical probability: Probability estimated by performing many trials and calculating relative frequency
- Law of Large Numbers: As the number of trials increases, simulated probabilities get closer to the true probability
- Independence: Knowing the result of one trial does not affect the probability of future trials
- Gambler's fallacy: The mistaken belief that past outcomes affect future probabilities

## Grading Criteria

### For "designDigits" (digit assignment):
- E (Essentially Correct): Correctly assigns digits to match the probability (e.g., 1-82 for 82% success, 83-100 for failure). Must mention specific digit ranges.
- P (Partially Correct): Shows understanding but is vague about specific ranges, or minor calculation error.
- I (Incorrect): Wrong digit assignment or doesn't match the probability.

### For "designTrial" (trial definition):
- E (Essentially Correct): Describes (1) what random numbers to generate, (2) when to stop or how many, (3) what to record. All three elements present.
- P (Partially Correct): Has 1-2 of the three elements but missing one key component.
- I (Incorrect): Does not clearly define what constitutes one trial.

Key elements for trial definition:
- What to generate: random numbers from 1-100, how many
- Stopping rule: when the trial ends (e.g., "until miss", "generate 5 numbers")
- Recording: what to count or note (e.g., "count successes", "record if 3+ correct")

### For "capExplain" (capstone explanation):
- E (Essentially Correct): Clearly explains WHY the chosen concept applies. Uses relevant vocabulary. Shows understanding of the relationship between the scenario and the concept.
- P (Partially Correct): Mentions the right concept but explanation is vague or incomplete.
- I (Incorrect): Misunderstands the concept or provides no valid reasoning.

Key concepts to look for:
- Independence: Each trial has the same probability regardless of past results. Past outcomes do NOT affect future probabilities.
- Gambler's fallacy: The WRONG belief that things will "even out" or that outcomes are "due."
- Law of Large Numbers: More trials → closer to true probability. Does NOT mean things balance out in the short run.
- Simulation: Model random events, perform many trials, calculate relative frequency.
- Streaks: Normal part of random variation. Humans avoid them when faking randomness.

Common misconceptions (mark as I if present):
- Saying the Law of Large Numbers means things "balance out" in the short run
- Believing past results affect future probability (gambler's fallacy presented as correct)
- Confusing outcome (single result) with event (collection of outcomes)
- Thinking streaks indicate non-randomness
