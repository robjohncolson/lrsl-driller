## Grading Criteria

### For "whyGeneralize" (explaining generalization):
- E (Essentially Correct): Student correctly explains that generalization depends on RANDOM SELECTION. Must mention one of: random selection, representative sample, or that every member had a chance to be selected.
- P (Partially Correct): Shows some understanding but is incomplete (e.g., mentions "random" without specifying selection, or mentions sample size instead of selection method)
- I (Incorrect): No mention of random selection, or fundamentally wrong reasoning (e.g., "because it's an experiment")

Key concepts for generalization:
- Random SELECTION from the population allows generalization
- Without random selection, sample may be biased/not representative
- Sample size alone does NOT determine generalizability

### For "whyCause" (explaining causation):
- E (Essentially Correct): Student correctly explains that causation depends on RANDOM ASSIGNMENT. Must mention one of: random assignment, confounding variables, lurking variables, or that treatments were assigned by researchers.
- P (Partially Correct): Shows some understanding but confuses selection with assignment, or mentions "random" without specifying what
- I (Incorrect): No mention of random assignment or confounding, or confuses with generalization

Key concepts for causation:
- Random ASSIGNMENT of treatments allows causal conclusions
- Without random assignment, confounding/lurking variables may explain differences
- Only experiments can have random assignment
- Observational studies can only show association, not causation

### For "advantage" (explaining method advantages):
- E: Correctly identifies a specific advantage of the method tied to the scenario (e.g., "ensures representation of all departments" for stratified, "more practical/cheaper" for cluster)
- P: Gives a generic advantage not tied to the scenario, or partially correct reasoning
- I: Wrong advantage for the method, or no valid reasoning


### For "biasExplain" (explaining sampling/response bias):
- E (Essentially Correct): Student explains the correct bias mechanism for the scenario and why it can make results unrepresentative.
  - Voluntary response: people choose to respond; often strong opinions are overrepresented.
  - Undercoverage: some groups are left out of the sampling frame; they have little/no chance to be selected.
  - Nonresponse: many selected individuals do not respond; nonrespondents may differ from respondents.
  - Response bias: wording/survey method/sensitive topic leads to inaccurate or systematically skewed answers (e.g., leading question, lying/misreporting).
- P (Partially Correct): Student notes the results may be biased/unrepresentative but is vague, only names the bias without explaining, or mixes up the bias type.
- I (Incorrect): No relevant explanation of bias mechanism, or explanation is fundamentally off-topic (e.g., talks about random assignment/causation).

Key vocabulary to look for:
- Voluntary response: "volunteer", "self-selected", "opt-in", "strong opinions"
- Undercoverage: "excluded", "left out", "not included", "sampling frame", "no chance"
- Nonresponse: "didn't respond", "refused", "low response rate"
- Response bias: "leading question", "wording", "confusing", "double-barreled", "lie", "misreport", "sensitive"

### For "methodExplain" (why this method is the best choice here):
- E: Student gives a scenario-tied advantage of the best method (representation, precision/variability, or practicality/cost/logistics).
  - Stratified: ensures each subgroup is represented; can reduce variability / improve precision.
  - Cluster: cheaper/more practical for spread-out populations; focuses data collection in fewer locations.
  - Systematic: random start then every kth; easy to implement for ordered/streaming populations.
  - SRS: simple/unbiased when a complete list exists and no subgroup structure is required.
- P: Student gives a generic reason ("it's random", "it's representative") without tying to method-specific features, OR reasoning fits a different method.
- I: No valid reasoning about why the method fits, or irrelevant/incorrect justification.

### For "largeExplain" (large sample vs representative sample misconception):
- E: Student clearly states that a large sample size does NOT guarantee representativeness/generalizability, and explains that random selection (and avoiding bias) is what matters. May note: larger samples reduce random error (improve precision) but do not fix bias.
- P: Student mentions precision/margin of error/sample size helps accuracy but does not explicitly address bias/random selection, or is unclear.
- I: Student supports the misconception (claims large sample guarantees representativeness) or gives no relevant reasoning.

### For sampling method identification:
- E: Correctly identifies the method AND shows understanding
- P: Correct method but weak reasoning, OR wrong method with partial understanding
- I: Wrong method with no valid reasoning

### Key vocabulary to look for:
- Generalization: "random selection," "representative," "every member had a chance," "sample represents population"
- Causation: "random assignment," "confounding," "lurking variables," "treatments assigned," "cause and effect"
- Stratified: "from each group," "ensures representation," "reduces variability"
- Cluster: "entire groups," "practical," "convenient," "cheaper"
- SRS: "equal chance," "every group of n"
