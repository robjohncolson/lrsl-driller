Key ideas for Topic 7.2:
- When the goal is to estimate the mean μ of a single population, the correct procedure is a one-sample t-interval for a population mean.
- Conditions for a one-sample t-interval:
  1. Random: data come from a random sample or randomized experiment.
  2. 10% condition: when sampling without replacement, n ≤ 10% of the population.
  3. Nearly Normal condition: either n ≥ 30, or if n < 30 the sample data show no strong skewness and no outliers.
- The critical value comes from a t distribution, not a standard normal distribution.
- Degrees of freedom are df = n - 1.
- Margin of error for a mean is t* × s / √n.
- A confidence interval for a population mean is x̄ ± t* × s / √n.

Common mistakes to detect:
- Choosing a proportion procedure instead of a mean procedure.
- Choosing a test instead of an interval.
- Using z* instead of t*.
- Forgetting df = n - 1.
- Forgetting the square root in the standard error.
- Using the standard error alone instead of multiplying by t* for margin of error.
- Swapping lower and upper bounds of the interval.
- For condition checks, forgetting one of the three required conditions.
- For small samples, saying "n < 30" automatically fails without checking skewness and outliers.
